Call for Contributions

VIS4DH'27 invites contributions in two ways: you can submit to the workshop's Paper track, or to the Agora track, which calls for lab talks, exhibits and provocations. Our call is open to all fields of the (digital) humanities and social sciences, and to all areas of visualization research and practice. The workshop is intended to put different ways of seeing, knowing, articulating, and transforming arguments into dialogue in order to foster and intensify collaborations between humanities and visualization researchers.

Scope

This year, VIS4DH does not have a specific workshop theme. Instead, we openly invite contributions related to visualization and the (digital) humanities. The focus is on visualization theory and practice in humanities research and, vice versa, humanities-centered theories and ideas in the context of visualization research. Workshop discussions focus on questions and unmet challenges that arise in this context.

The workshop targets visualization-related research and work from domains such as Literature, Philology and Linguistics, Art History and Art Criticism, Political Science, Philosophy, Media Studies, Performance (Music, Dance, Film, and Theatre), and Cultural and Historical Studies.

Paper Track

For our paper track, we seek works from scholars in visualization, the humanities, social science, and the arts who use visualization as part of analyzing and interrogating human culture. Submissions present original research ideas and arguments, findings, and works-in-progress at the intersection of visualization and the (digital) humanities.

Submission format

Papers (4–5 pages) describe and critically discuss work at the intersection of visualization and humanities research, including applied case studies, empirical results, and/or theoretical perspectives. We welcome works highlighting the difficulties (and proposed solutions) of designing visualizations in the context of humanities research and/or applying concepts from humanities research to foster visualization research and design.

Review and publication

Submissions will be peer-reviewed by members of the organizing committee and the invited program committee (see Organizers), and authors will receive detailed reviews. Submitted papers are expected to have archival quality. All accepted papers will be archived on arXiv, and we plan to additionally publish them in the EG Digital Library.

Authors of accepted papers will be invited to present at the workshop. Presentations are short (10 minutes each) to leave more space for community exchange, and a respondent from the community may be nominated for each paper to trigger on-site discussion.

Agora Track

In addition to papers, the agora track invites more open, discursive contributions — inspired by the atmosphere of ancient Greek marketplaces where citizens gathered to exchange ideas, challenge perspectives, and shape the future. Submissions (1 page) can take different forms:

  • Lab Talks are proposed by members of a research group, lab, or community doing work at the intersection of visualization and the (digital) humanities. Groups will be included in the VIS4DH lab overview, highlighting the growing VIS4DH network.
  • Exhibits present works-in-progress in a visual and/or hands-on way.
  • Provocations take the form of 200-word arguments (along with optional 100-word counter-arguments) addressing a prompting question.

Accepted agora talks are very short (3–5 minutes each) and focus on community discussion. Agora track submissions will be hosted on the workshop website.
